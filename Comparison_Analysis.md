# Nanobubble Generation Methods Compared (나노버블 생성 방식 비교)

_Last updated: 2026-11-12._

## 1. Methods

| Method | How bubbles form | Typical notes |
|---|---|---|
| Surface friction (FAWOO) | Water and gas flow through a channel; wall friction stretches and splits bubbles repeatedly | No moving parts in the flow-path type; scales by adding modules |
| Venturi / pressurized dissolution | Gas dissolved under pressure, released through a nozzle | Common and low cost; output often includes many microbubbles |
| High-pressure jet / vortex | Gas sheared in a high-speed jet or swirl | Higher pump energy |
| Rotor-stator mixer | Mechanical shear between a rotor and a stator | Moving parts and wear |
| Porous membrane | Gas pushed through fine pores | Membrane fouling; usually small flow |
| Electrolysis | Gas generated at electrodes | Limited to H₂ and O₂; electrode upkeep |

The notes column is the company's own assessment of the method types, not a measured comparison
of named products.

## 2. How to compare nanobubble claims (수치 비교 시 확인할 것)

A concentration or size figure means little without its conditions. Check:

1. **Instrument:** NTA (nanoparticle tracking analysis, e.g. NanoSight) counts individual particles.
   DLS (dynamic light scattering) reports an average size, not a count.
2. **Dilution:** if the sample was diluted before measurement, was the reported value multiplied
   back? An extrapolated value is an estimate, not a reading.
3. **Baseline:** the same instrument run on the source water before treatment. Tap water contains
   particles too.
4. **Statistic:** mean, mode or D50 (median) size. They differ.
5. **Time after generation:** measured immediately, or after hours or days of storage.
6. **Gas and temperature:** results change with the gas used and with water temperature.

FAWOO's published figures and their conditions are in [README.md](README.md).
